# Competitive benchmark (LE-11, full protocol) - 2026-11-22

libembedding against SentenceTransformers and FastEmbed on the same machine, the same
corpus and the same protocol. Script: `bench_competitive.py`. Raw data: `results.json`
(one entry per cell). Every per-batch, per-thread and per-library table:
[`results_tables.md`](results_tables.md) (generated by `bench_competitive.py report`).
Method notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

## Update 2026-11-28: ONNX dense remeasured with 1.17

The libembedding ONNX dense rows of the 2026-11-22 campaign below are **void**. Until 1.16.7,
the Python binding built its options from a zero-filled struct. `batch_strategy` 0 is
`LEMBED_BATCH_SEQUENTIAL`, so every text ran in its own ORT call and `batch_size` was ignored.
The cause is LE-11 E7, fixed in 1.16.7 (#132). That explains the flat curve from batch 1 to
128 and the 6 to 10 % gap at batch 32. The GGUF, sparse and quality rows are not affected.

The dense ONNX cells were remeasured with the same protocol and corpus. Two things changed:
- code: `dev` at 463c5de, the 1.17.0 content, Release build;
- competitor versions: sentence-transformers 6.3.0, torch 2.15.0 (CPU), fastembed 0.9.1,
  onnxruntime 1.31.0 in the venv.

Raw data: [`results_2026-11-28.json`](results_2026-11-28.json). Tables:
[`results_tables_2026-11-28.md`](results_tables_2026-11-28.md). Quality was not rerun:
batching does not move FP32 vectors (cosine >= 0.99999 against one text at a time, checked
by `python/tests/test_onnx_dense_batching.py`).

Batch 32, default threads (4), docs/s, median of 3 rounds:

| Model | libembedding FP32 | libembedding INT8 | SentenceTransformers | FastEmbed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all-MiniLM-L6-v2 | **502** (was 312) | not measured | 324 | 324 |
| bge-small-en-v1.5 | **265** (was 162) | 252 (was 263) | 170 | 142 (FP16 file) |

How to read it:
- libembedding FP32 is now ahead at batch 32 on both models: +55 % against
  SentenceTransformers on MiniLM and bge-small, +55 % against FastEmbed on MiniLM, and +87 %
  against FastEmbed on bge-small. FastEmbed's bge-small file holds FP16 weights. On the same
  FP16 file, libembedding gives 148 against 142: parity.
- Batching now pays off. libembedding FP32 grows from 309 docs/s at batch 1 to 544 at batch 64
  on MiniLM, and from 161 to 265 on bge-small. Batch 1 still gives 3.0 to 3.3x the throughput
  of SentenceTransformers.
- INT8 dynamic runs one text per call since 1.17 (T46, #133): its vectors no longer depend on
  their batch neighbours. Its throughput is therefore flat (236 to 270 docs/s from batch 1 to
  128). FP32 batched is now as fast as INT8 on these short texts.
- 8 threads on 4 cores still slows ORT down: MiniLM 360 against 502, bge-small 218 against 265.
  4 threads, the default, remains the right setting.
- Load time and peak memory are unchanged: 0.6 to 0.9 s and 204 to 247 MB for FP32 ONNX.

Gaps above 20 %, with their explanations:
- libembedding FP32 against FastEmbed at batch 32. The MiniLM file is FP32 for both libraries,
  so this is not profiled. A plausible cause is length bucketing: libembedding sorts texts by
  length before batching and pads each batch to its own longest text. A short-text corpus
  with mixed lengths favours that.
- libembedding against SentenceTransformers at batch 1: the per-call torch overhead, as in the
  2026-11-22 reading below.

## Reading

- FP32 ONNX at batch 32 on short texts: libembedding is 6 to 10 % behind SentenceTransformers
  and FastEmbed on MiniLM (312 against 333 and 346) and 9 % behind SentenceTransformers on
  bge-small (162 against 177), 6 % ahead of FastEmbed there. These gaps are within 20 %.
- FastEmbed's bge-small file is a 66 MB FP16-weight export, not FP32. Loading the same file
  through libembedding gives 143 docs/s against FastEmbed's 152 (-6 %, headline cell flagged
  disturbed): parity. The INT8 row (34 MB) is a different file and is the fastest at 263
  docs/s, at a quality cost of cosine 0.9976 to the FP32 reference (pair-rank agreement 0.998).
- Batch 1 is where libembedding stands out: MiniLM 321 docs/s against 97 for
  SentenceTransformers (3.3x) and 287 for FastEmbed (+12 %); bge-small 163 against 54 (3.0x)
  and 150 (+9 %). libembedding is flat from batch 1 to 128 (312 to 325 on MiniLM);
  SentenceTransformers needs batch 32 to reach its peak; FastEmbed loses throughput at large
  batches on bge-small (173 at batch 8, 129 at batch 128), so libembedding FP32 is 25 % ahead
  of it at batch 128.
- Load time: 0.4 to 1.2 s for libembedding against 0.9 to 1.1 s for FastEmbed and 8.6 s for
  SentenceTransformers. Peak memory: 201-244 MB (FP32 ONNX), 78-89 MB (GGUF), 103 MB (INT8)
  against 228-268 MB (FastEmbed) and 532-569 MB (SentenceTransformers).
- Sparse: SPLADE++ is 57 docs/s in libembedding against 33 in FastEmbed (+75 %), with 795 MB
  against 1149 MB. bge-m3 sparse has no competitor: FastEmbed's sparse models are SPLADE++,
  BM25, BM42, miniCOIL and an opensearch model, and SentenceTransformers' `SparseEncoder`
  covers SPLADE-type models only.
- Quality: with documents cut to 100 words (no library truncates), libembedding FP32 and
  FastEmbed match SentenceTransformers to cosine 1.0000 and GGUF Q8_0 to 0.9997-0.9999. The
  Spearman of pair scores against the labels is the same to three digits (0.671 MiniLM,
  0.628 bge-small), so no retrieval quality is lost against the reference.
- Threads: 8 threads on 4 cores is slower for libembedding (MiniLM 254 against 312; bge-small
  126 against 162) and FastEmbed (323 against 346, 138 against 152), flat for
  SentenceTransformers. 4 threads equals the default. Details in `results_tables.md`.

## Gaps above 20 % and their explanations

Gaps on the headline settings (batch 1 and 32, default threads); every cell is in
`results.json`.

| Gap | Explanation | Status |
|---|---|---|
| libembedding FP32 / INT8 vs SentenceTransformers at batch 1 (+165 to +400 %) | torch's per-call overhead and poor use of 4 cores on one sequence; SentenceTransformers needs batch 32 to peak (54 to 97 docs/s at batch 1 against 177 to 333 at batch 32) | Plausible, not profiled |
| libembedding INT8 vs FastEmbed (+73 % at batch 32, +82 % at batch 1) | not like for like: INT8 against a FP16-weight file; the like-for-like FP16 row is within 6 % | Verified by the FP16 row |
| libembedding vs FastEmbed on bge-small at batch 128 (+25 %) | FastEmbed slows down at large batches (173 docs/s at batch 8 to 129 at batch 128); probably padding to the longest text of a large batch | Hypothesis, not profiled |
| GGUF Q8_0 vs the ONNX / torch paths (-32 to -46 %) | GGUF is flat at about 195 (MiniLM) and 100 docs/s (bge-small) at every batch size: `LlamaSession::embed_batch` loops over the texts, one `llama_encode` per text, so batching never helps; Q8_0 is also slower than FP32 on this CPU | Loop read in `include/libembedding/detail/llama_session_impl.hpp`, not profiled |
| SPLADE++ libembedding vs FastEmbed (+75 %) | probably the post-processing (logits to sparse weights) in C against Python / numpy, and 354 MB less memory | Hypothesis, not profiled |
| libembedding at 8 threads vs SentenceTransformers / FastEmbed (-22 to -31 %, batch >= 32) | oversubscription: 8 threads on 4 cores penalises ORT (312 to 254) but not torch (flat); probably ORT spin-waiting. Not a default-path issue since the default is 4 threads | Hypothesis, not profiled |

## Findings (bugs and gaps found while preparing the campaign)

1. **Qwen3-Embedding GGUF dense crashes** (access violation, exit 0xC0000005) at the first
   `embed` call; the model loads. The dense GGUF path only calls `llama_encode`
   (encoder architectures), so decoder-only embedding models are not supported. The five
   Qwen3 cells are recorded as crashes in `results.json` and were not retried. Qwen3 is
   therefore measured on SentenceTransformers only.
2. **Dense GGUF aborts the process above 512 tokens**:
   `GGML_ASSERT(cparams.n_ubatch >= n_tokens)`. `LlamaSession::tokenize` ignores
   `max_tokens` and the `max_length` option is stored but never applied
   (`text_embedding_llama_impl.hpp`). The `full` quality variant skips GGUF for that reason;
   the `cut` variant (100 words) is the one GGUF can run.
3. **libembedding's MiniLM default `max_length` is 512, SentenceTransformers' is 256**
   (`sentence_bert_config.json`). On whole SciFact abstracts libembedding's vectors differ
   from the reference (cosine mean 0.984, min 0.875, pair-score Spearman 0.993) while
   FastEmbed matches it (1.0000). bge-small (512 in both) matches. This is a
   default-behaviour difference users will see on long texts, not a numerical error.
4. **MaxSim kernel and `maxsim_batch`** (see `benchmarks/multivector/results.md`):
   `include/libembedding/similarity.h:131-145` is a scalar dependent-chain loop, not
   vectorised without fast-math, against numpy's BLAS matrix product; and
   `python/src/libembedding/similarity.py:96-98` concatenates all documents on every call
   (about 90 MB copy on long documents), so `maxsim_batch` is slower than a `maxsim` loop.
   Fixes are being done in other branches; those files are untouched here.

## Limits and disturbed cells

- One machine (i7-1065G7, 4 cores, 16 GB); no conclusion for ARM, AMD or more cores.
- Each library uses its own model file and defaults (pooling, maximum length), so the
  comparison is the out-of-the-box experience, not identical graphs. GGUF is Q8_0, the rest
  FP32 except where stated.
- **Disturbed cells** (within-process spread of the 3 passes, max-min over mean, above 10 %):
  36 of the 306 completed cells. They stay in the tables and should be read with their
  range. Headline cells concerned: MiniLM SentenceTransformers round 1 (14 %), MiniLM
  libembedding round 3 (18 %), bge-small INT8 round 2 (11 %), bge-m3 sparse round 2 (12 %),
  bge-small FP16 file round 1 (11 %; its rounds range from 121 to 148). The others are almost
  all 8-thread cells (4 cores, SMT oversubscription), MiniLM batch 1 and small-batch INT8 /
  FastEmbed cells. The lead asked to free the machine rather than re-run, so none was re-run.
  The full list is derivable from the `samples` field of `results.json`.
- The FP16-file row (`libembedding-fp16`) was added after the main run to fix a labelling
  error (FastEmbed's bge-small file is FP16-weight, not INT8) and was interrupted when the
  machine was needed: only 7 of its 31 cells exist (the 3 headline rounds, batch 1 at the
  three thread settings, batch 8 at default threads) and it has no quality row. It was
  measured after the main run, not rotated with the other libraries.
- Quality is measured on SciFact abstracts, throughput on short customer sentences:
  different distributions.
- Qwen3: libembedding not measured (crash); SentenceTransformers on 100 texts only.
- `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`: no network during the run; models were pre-cached.
